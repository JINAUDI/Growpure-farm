The Kushalgarh Estate

Where hospitality coexists with ecological sensitivity

1. Vision & Philosophy

The Kushalgarh Estate was founded with the belief that hospitality can coexist with ecological sensitivity. We operate with a minimal footprint philosophy: to preserve the natural character of the land, support the local community, and create meaningful nature-based experiences for guests. Sustainability is not an added component of the property; it is the foundation on which its architecture, operations, and cultural environment are built.

2. Location & Ecological Context

The Kushalgarh Estate is located beside a natural perennial stream, surrounded by evergreen forest and hill terrain with multiple seasonal water bodies. The property is having over 100 acres of untouched natural forest, forming a biodiverse habitat that supports numerous species of birds, butterflies, amphibians, and small wild mammals. We prioritize the conservation of this ecological corridor and maintain designated sanctuary zones where no construction or landscaping is done.

3. Current Sustainability Practices

A. Energy Efficiency
  • Use of solar-powered 7.5 HP water pump reduces grid dependence.
  • Two additional government-approved solar units of 7.5 HP each are scheduled for installation. 30KW Hybrid Solar Power Plant for power generation and backup.
  • Low-energy LED lighting is used across the property.
  • Passive cooling architecture significantly reduces the need for air-conditioning and heating.
B. Water Conservation and Natural Hydrology
  • Rainwater harvesting, channelisation, desilting of waterbodies, regular maintenance of check dams. All done scientifically with drone survey, terrain mapping & watershed modelling.
  • A natural pond with wetland-style filtration manages greywater naturally.
  • Drip irrigation & sprinklers ensures optimal water use for landscaping.
  • The Property embraces water as a shared ecological asset, not a commercial utility.
C. Circular Waste Management
  • Segregation at source practiced for all waste streams.
  • ~3000 to 3500 kg of compost monthly, used in gardens and agriculture.
  • Glass, metal, and cloth are reused or repurposed.
  • Strict plastic-free policy for guest and staff operations.
D. Sustainable Construction
  • Buildings use local stone, clay, mud-plaster, and bamboo, minimizing embodied carbon.
  • Minimal use of concrete preserves soil permeability.
  • Structures blend into natural contours rather than reshape the landscape.
E. Sustainable Food Systems
  • Organic kitchen garden supports a farm-to-table food system.
  • Locally sourced vegetables, grains, fruits & poultry reduce supply chain emissions.
  • No packaged drinking water; purified refill stations provided.
  • Zero-waste kitchen practices ensure food resources are cycled back into soil.
F. Local Community Integration
  • ~90% of staff are from nearby villages.
  • Local artisans invited to display and sell crafts within the resort.
  • Nature walks guided by trained local residents.
  • Training local youth in eco-hospitality and biodiversity interpretation.
G. Biodiversity Stewardship
  • Native trees prioritized in landscaping.
  • Butterfly and bird-friendly habitat maintained without chemical inputs.
  • Portions of land intentionally left wild as wildlife corridors.

6. Conclusion

The Kushalgarh Estate represents a living model of regenerative hospitality, where tourism supports ecological balance, local livelihoods, and cultural continuity.

  • Authentic sustainability in daily operations
  • Community-integrated development
  • Protection of natural landscapes and biodiversity
  • Scalable and replicable eco-hospitality practices

The Kushalgarh Estate is committed to deepening its sustainability footprint and evolving as a reference ecosystem for nature-based rural tourism.
